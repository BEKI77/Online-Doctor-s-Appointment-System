Abstract
The increasing adoption of information and communication technologies offers new
opportunities to improve healthcare service delivery, yet many healthcare facilities still rely on
manual appointment scheduling methods, leading to long waiting times, scheduling conflicts,
inefficient record keeping, and limited communication between patients and healthcare
providers. To address these challenges, this project proposes the design and future
implementation of a web-based online doctor’s appointment system that will streamline
interactions among patients, doctors, and administrative staff. The system will allow patients to
register online, manage their profiles, view available medical services, and schedule
appointments at convenient times, while also supporting virtual consultations through Google
Meet integration for remote access. It will operate using three main user roles: patient, doctor,
and admin enabling doctors to manage their availability and appointments and administrators to
oversee appointment approvals and system management. Developed with Next.js and TypeScript
for the frontend, Node.js and Express.js for the backend, and PostgreSQL for secure data storage,
the system will implement authentication via JSON Web Tokens and secure integration with
Google services through OAuth 2.0. With a focus on usability, security, scalability, and
performance, the system is expected to reduce administrative workload, enhance patient
engagement, and improve communication between patients and healthcare providers, ultimately
contributing to more efficient healthcare service delivery in the future.

1. Introduction
Healthcare services in many areas face challenges such as limited infrastructure, high patient
demand, and reliance on manual appointment scheduling and record-keeping. These traditional
methods often lead to long waiting times, scheduling conflicts, incomplete records, and poor
communication between patients and healthcare providers. The lack of digital solutions further
limits access and efficiency, particularly in remote or underserved areas.
This project proposes the design and future implementation of a web-based online doctor’s
appointment system. The system will allow patients to register online, manage profiles, view
services, and schedule appointments. It will support virtual consultations through Google Meet
and include role-based access for patients, doctors, and administrators. The system aims to

1.1 Background
Healthcare systems in many developing countries face challenges such as limited infrastructure,
high patient demand, and inefficient administrative processes. In Ethiopia, these challenges are
particularly evident, as many healthcare facilities still rely on manual methods for managing
patient appointments and medical records, including paper-based registration and phone calls.
Such traditional approaches often lead to long waiting times, scheduling conflicts, incomplete or
lost records, and poor communication between patients and healthcare providers. The limited use
of digital technologies further restricts the accessibility and quality of healthcare, especially for
patients in remote or underserved areas.
The growing need for efficient, timely, and well-organized healthcare services highlights the
potential of digital solutions to address these challenges. Web-based appointment systems can
streamline patient registration, scheduling, and record management while improving
communication between patients and healthcare providers. By reducing administrative workload,

minimizing errors, and providing timely notifications, such systems can enhance patient
satisfaction and overall service delivery, even in resource-limited settings.
This project proposes the design and future implementation of a web-based online doctor’s
appointment system tailored to the Ethiopian healthcare context. The system will enable patients
to register online, manage their personal profiles, view available medical services, and schedule
appointments efficiently. It will also support virtual consultations through Google Meet
integration, improving access for patients who cannot easily visit healthcare facilities. By
implementing role-based access for patients, doctors, and administrators, the system aims to
enhance appointment management, streamline communication, and contribute to a more
organized, accessible, and efficient healthcare service delivery in Ethiopia.

1.2 Statement of the Problem
Despite the critical role of healthcare services, many facilities continue to rely on manual and
paper-based processes for patient registration, appointment scheduling, and record management.
These traditional methods are often slow, inefficient, and prone to human error, resulting in long
waiting times, missed or overlapping appointments, and incomplete patient records. Patients
frequently face difficulties in accessing information about available services or doctors, while
healthcare staff spend considerable time on administrative tasks that could otherwise be devoted
to patient care.
Additionally, the lack of a centralized digital system makes it difficult to coordinate schedules,
track patient history, and provide timely notifications or reminders for upcoming consultations.
This gap not only affects the efficiency of healthcare providers but also reduces patient
satisfaction and engagement, creating challenges for maintaining quality care. The growing
demand for healthcare services further exacerbates these issues, making manual systems
increasingly inadequate to meet the needs of both patients and providers.
Therefore, there is a pressing need for a reliable, user-friendly, and accessible digital system that
can streamline appointment management, improve communication between patients and

healthcare staff, and reduce administrative workload. Such a system would allow patients to
book and manage appointments conveniently, while enabling healthcare providers to monitor
schedules, track patient history, and offer timely services more effectively. Implementing a
web-based solution with virtual consultation capabilities and role-based access would address
these challenges, improving overall healthcare service delivery and patient experience.

2. Requirement Analysis Description
2.1 Overview of the Existing System
Currently, most healthcare facilities rely on manual processes to manage patient appointments
and records. Patients typically register in person at the reception desk, where their personal and
medical information is recorded in physical logbooks or spreadsheets. Appointment schedules
are often maintained manually, creating challenges in coordinating patient visits, avoiding
conflicts, and tracking history.
Communication between patients and healthcare providers is mostly handled through phone calls
or in-person inquiries, which can lead to missed information or delays. Doctors and

administrative staff spend considerable time managing these manual processes, which reduces
the time available for direct patient care.
While this system has the advantage of being familiar to staff and low-cost, it is inefficient,
error-prone, and difficult to scale, especially as patient volumes increase or when virtual
consultations are needed. The lack of centralized digital management makes it difficult to track
patient history, monitor appointments, and provide timely notifications or reminders.

2.1.1 Activities of the System
The current manual system for managing healthcare appointments involves several key activities
performed by patients, doctors, and administrative staff. These activities include:
➢​ Patient Registration​
○​ Patients provide their personal and medical information at the reception.​
○​ Administrative staff record this information manually in logbooks or
spreadsheets.​
➢​ Appointment Scheduling​
○​ Patients request appointments in person or via phone.​
○​ Staff manually check the doctor’s availability and assign time slots.​
○​ Appointment details are written in physical appointment books or basic
spreadsheets.​
➢​ Consultation Management​
○​ Doctors review appointment schedules prepared by staff.​

○​ Consultation notes and patient records are maintained on paper.​
○​ Follow-ups are recorded manually, requiring staff to track patient visits.​
➢​ Communication and Notifications​
○​ Appointment confirmations and reminders are given verbally or through phone
calls.​
○​ Patients may miss appointments due to lack of automated reminders.​
➢​ Record Maintenance and Reporting​
○​ Staff are responsible for maintaining patient records, appointment history, and
doctor schedules.​
○​ Generating reports or monitoring trends is time-consuming and prone to errors
due to the manual process.
○​

2.1.2 Problems of the Existing System
➢​ Long waiting times due to manual registration and scheduling processes.​
➢​ Scheduling conflicts caused by the lack of a centralized system.​
➢​ Incomplete, lost, or damaged patient records because of paper-based documentation.​
➢​ Limited patient access to appointment information, making scheduling inconvenient.​

➢​ Communication gaps, as reminders and notifications are handled verbally or via phone
calls.​
➢​ High administrative workload for staff managing appointments, records, and follow-ups.​
➢​ Inefficient reporting and monitoring due to reliance on manual record-keeping.

2.1.3 Weaknesses and Strengths
Weaknesses
➢​ The manual appointment system is time-consuming, requiring staff to handle registration,
scheduling, and record-keeping physically, which slows down the overall workflow.​
➢​ Scheduling conflicts are frequent because there is no centralized platform to coordinate
patient appointments, often resulting in double bookings or missed appointments.​
➢​ Patient records are prone to loss, damage, or incompleteness due to reliance on
paper-based documentation, making it difficult to maintain accurate medical history.​
➢​ Communication between patients and healthcare staff is limited and inefficient, as
reminders, confirmations, and notifications are usually conveyed verbally or through
phone calls.​
➢​ Administrative staff are burdened with repetitive tasks, reducing the time available for
direct patient care and increasing the likelihood of human error.​
➢​ The system lacks scalability; as the number of patients increases, managing appointments
and records becomes increasingly difficult and inefficient.​

Strengths
➢​ The current system is simple and familiar to staff, requiring minimal training to operate.​
➢​ It has a low initial cost since it does not rely on digital infrastructure or complex
technology.​
➢​ Basic appointment management is achievable without depending on internet access or
electronic devices.​
➢​ Staff have direct control over records and schedules, which can provide a sense of
oversight in small facilities.

2.1.4 Business Rules
1.​ Each patient must register with a unique identifier to ensure proper tracking of
appointments and medical history.​

This ensures that every patient can be uniquely recognized by the system. It prevents
confusion between patients with similar names and allows the system to maintain
accurate records of appointments, consultations, and medical history. Unique identifiers
also make it easier for administrators to manage data and generate reports.​
2.​ Appointments requested by patients must be approved or confirmed by the
administrator before they are finalized.​
This rule ensures that appointments are properly scheduled and coordinated with the
doctor’s availability. Administrators can prevent double bookings or conflicts, ensuring
that patients receive confirmed slots and that doctors’ schedules remain organized. It also
allows staff to intervene in case of errors or special requests.​

3.​ Administrators have full access to manage patient and doctor records, approve or
reject appointments, and oversee overall system operations.​
Administrators act as the system managers, ensuring smooth operations. They can
modify records, handle scheduling issues, and monitor system activity. This centralized
control helps maintain accuracy, resolve conflicts, and provide support to both patients
and doctors when needed.​
4.​ Virtual consultations are only accessible through automatically generated Google
Meet links, which are provided to patients upon confirmation of their appointment.​
To ensure security and convenience, virtual consultation links are generated
automatically by the system and sent to patients after their appointment is confirmed.
This prevents unauthorized access and simplifies the process for patients and doctors,
allowing them to join consultations without manual link sharing.​
5.​ Patients are responsible for providing accurate personal and contact information
during registration to ensure proper scheduling and communication.​
Accurate patient information is critical for effective appointment management. Correct
contact details allow the system to send notifications, reminders, and virtual consultation
links. Errors or omissions can lead to missed appointments, confusion, or communication
breakdowns.​
6.​ All appointments, records, and notifications must be stored securely to protect
patient privacy and comply with data protection standards.​
Sensitive data must be encrypted and stored safely to prevent unauthorized access.
Secure storage ensures compliance with privacy regulations, protects patient
confidentiality, and maintains trust between patients, doctors, and administrators.​
7.​ System notifications and reminders must be sent automatically to patients and
doctors to minimize missed appointments and improve communication.​
Automated notifications reduce human error and ensure that all parties are informed
about upcoming appointments. Reminders help patients attend consultations on time and

allow doctors to prepare for scheduled visits, improving the overall efficiency of the
healthcare service.

2.2 Overview of the Proposed System
The proposed system is a web-based online doctor’s appointment platform designed to overcome
the limitations of the existing manual system. It provides a centralized, digital solution for
managing patient registration, appointment scheduling, and communication between patients,
doctors, and administrators. The system aims to increase efficiency, reduce administrative
workload, and improve patient experience.

➢​ Patient Registration and Profile Management​
Patients can create accounts with personal, contact, and basic medical information. This
ensures that patient records are properly maintained, easy to access, and can be used to
schedule appointments and track medical history accurately.​
➢​ Role-Based Access Control​
The system distinguishes between patients, doctors, and administrators, giving each role
specific permissions. This ensures security, prevents unauthorized access, and allows
users to perform only the tasks relevant to their responsibilities.​
➢​ Appointment Scheduling​
Patients can view available services, select doctors, and book appointments online. The
system checks for scheduling conflicts and prevents double bookings, making the process
faster, more accurate, and convenient.​
➢​ Virtual Consultation Support (Google Meet Integration)​
Automatically generated Google Meet links are provided to patients and doctors for
remote consultations. This allows patients to access medical services from home or

distant locations, improving accessibility and reducing the need for physical visits.​
➢​ Automated Notifications and Reminders​
The system sends reminders to patients and doctors about upcoming appointments or
follow-ups. This reduces missed appointments, keeps users informed, and improves
overall communication.​
➢​ Display of Service Fees and Payment Methods​
Patients can view the costs of services and available payment options. Although the
system does not handle direct transactions, this feature helps patients plan their visits and
reduces confusion about fees.​
➢​ Admin Dashboard​
Administrators can approve or reject appointments, manage patient and doctor accounts,
and monitor overall system activity. The dashboard provides centralized control to ensure
smooth operation and maintain data accuracy.​
➢​ Patient Portal and Feedback​
Patients can access their appointment history, view records, and provide feedback on
services. This feature promotes patient engagement and allows healthcare providers to
improve service quality.

2.2.2 Non-Functional Requirements

➢​ Usability​
The system must have a simple, intuitive, and user-friendly interface that can be easily
used by patients with varying levels of technical literacy. Clear navigation,
understandable instructions, and a responsive design are essential to encourage adoption
and minimize errors during usage.​

➢​ Security​
Patient data and appointment records must be protected from unauthorized access. The
system will implement password encryption, role-based access control, and secure
session management. Additionally, sensitive information such as personal and medical
data must comply with privacy standards to ensure confidentiality and trust.​
➢​ Reliability and Availability​
The system must be available 24/7 with minimal downtime. It should provide consistent
performance under normal and peak usage, ensuring that patients can schedule
appointments and access information at any time. Backup and recovery mechanisms
should be in place to prevent data loss.​
➢​ Scalability​
The system should be capable of handling multiple concurrent users, including patients,
doctors, and administrators. It must efficiently manage growing amounts of data and
increasing numbers of users without performance degradation.​
➢​ Performance​
The system must respond quickly to user actions, such as logging in, scheduling
appointments, or accessing virtual consultation links. Fast response times are critical for
maintaining user satisfaction and preventing delays in healthcare service delivery.​
➢​ Maintainability​
The system should be designed in a modular and organized way, allowing developers to
update, fix, or enhance features without affecting the overall system. Proper
documentation and clear code structure will support maintainability.​
➢​ Portability​
The system must be accessible via different devices and web browsers, including
desktops, laptops, and mobile devices. This ensures flexibility for patients and staff to use
the system from various locations.

2.2.3 System Requirements (Hardware and Software)
The proposed online doctor’s appointment system requires both hardware and software
components to function effectively and provide a seamless experience for patients, doctors, and
administrators. On the hardware side, the system is designed to be practical and accessible,
requiring only standard computers or laptops with internet access. Patients can access the system
using personal computers, laptops, or mobile devices with modern web browsers. Healthcare
staff and administrators will also use standard office computers to manage appointments, patient
information, and system operations. The server, which hosts the application and database, should
be capable of handling multiple simultaneous users and storing patient records securely, but no
specialized or high-end hardware is necessary. This makes the system feasible for healthcare
facilities of varying sizes, including small clinics with limited resources.
On the software side, the system will use a layered web application architecture to ensure
performance, security, and scalability. The frontend will be developed using Next.js with
TypeScript, which allows the creation of a responsive, interactive, and user-friendly interface.
This ensures that patients can easily navigate the system, book appointments, and access virtual
consultations, while doctors and administrators can efficiently manage schedules and records.
The backend will be implemented using Node.js with Express.js, providing a fast and efficient
platform to handle application logic, API requests, and integration with external services such as
Google Meet. PostgreSQL will serve as the relational database management system, ensuring
reliable storage of patient records, appointment schedules, and system logs with high data
integrity.
Security is a key requirement of the system, and it will be ensured using JSON Web Tokens
(JWT) for secure authentication, session management, and role-based access control. OAuth 2.0
will be used for integrating Google Meet for virtual consultations, ensuring that only authorized
users can access online appointments. RESTful APIs will facilitate smooth communication
between the frontend and backend, enabling modular development and easy maintenance. The
software stack has been chosen to ensure the system is scalable, secure, and capable of handling
future enhancements without requiring major changes.

2.3 Constraints and Assumptions
The development and operation of the proposed online doctor’s appointment system are subject
to several constraints and assumptions that define its scope and ensure realistic expectations. One
of the main constraints is that the system will not handle direct online payment processing. While
it can display service fees and available payment methods, all transactions must be completed
outside the system, which simplifies development and reduces potential security risks. Another
constraint is that virtual consultations rely on stable internet connectivity; both patients and
doctors must have access to a reliable network to participate in online appointments.
Additionally, the system is focused on appointment management, scheduling, and
record-keeping, and does not include modules for diagnosing, prescribing medication, or other
advanced medical functionalities.
Several assumptions are made regarding system users and operations. It is assumed that patients
have basic familiarity with using web browsers or mobile devices, allowing them to navigate the
platform, register, and schedule appointments independently. Doctors and administrators are
expected to maintain accurate schedules, update availability, and ensure that patient information
is entered correctly. It is also assumed that patients will provide accurate personal and contact
details during registration, enabling proper scheduling, notifications, and virtual consultation
access. Furthermore, the system assumes that the healthcare facility provides adequate
infrastructure, such as computers and internet access, to support the platform’s operations.
These constraints and assumptions provide a framework for the development and deployment of
the system, ensuring that it operates effectively within practical limitations while meeting the
needs of patients, doctors, and administrators. By clearly defining these boundaries, the system
can be designed, implemented, and maintained in a manner that is both efficient and sustainable.

\section*{Abstract}
The integration of digital technology in healthcare has transformed how dermatology services and pharmacy operations are managed. This project proposes an "Online Dermatology Platform with an Integrated Online Pharmacy" to streamline patient care, from diagnosis to medicine procurement. The platform allows patients to register online, select specialized dermatology services, book appointments, and pay registration fees. Additionally, it features an online pharmacy module where patients can browse and purchase medicines, facilitating a complete healthcare cycle. Developed using Next.js, Node.js, and PostgreSQL, the system ensures secure data management and efficient communication between patients, dermatologists, pharmacists, and administrators. By automating registration, reminders, and payments, the system reduces administrative overhead and improves accessibility to especializados skincare and medication.

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Introduction}
The healthcare sector is undergoing a profound transformation driven by the rapid advancement of digital technologies and increased internet penetration. Web-based healthcare platforms have emerged as powerful tools for improving service delivery, enhancing patient engagement, and reducing operational inefficiencies. These systems enable healthcare providers to move beyond traditional, paper-based workflows and adopt data-driven, patient-centered models of care.

Dermatology, as a visually intensive medical specialty, is particularly well-suited for digital transformation. Many dermatological conditions can be assessed through clinical images, patient-reported symptoms, and medical history without requiring immediate physical examination. At the same time, access to dermatologists remains limited in many developing regions, including Ethiopia, where specialist care is often centralized in urban areas.

This project focuses on the intersection of dermatology and pharmacy services by proposing a unified digital platform that integrates online dermatological consultation with an online pharmacy. The goal is to provide an end-to-end healthcare solution that supports patients from initial consultation through diagnosis, prescription, and medication procurement. By leveraging modern web technologies, the platform aims to improve accessibility, efficiency, transparency, and quality of dermatological care.



\subsection{Background}
Healthcare systems in developing countries face persistent challenges related to limited infrastructure, workforce shortages, and inefficient service delivery mechanisms. Specialized medical services such as dermatology are often unavailable outside major cities, forcing patients to travel long distances for consultation and treatment. This situation disproportionately affects rural populations, leading to delayed diagnosis, increased financial burden, and reduced treatment adherence.

In Ethiopia, dermatological services are typically delivered through hospital outpatient departments and private clinics, many of which rely on manual record-keeping and in-person consultations. After receiving a prescription, patients must independently locate pharmacies, often encountering issues such as medication unavailability, long queues, or counterfeit drugs. This fragmented approach to healthcare delivery results in inefficiencies, duplication of effort, and poor continuity of care.

Recent advancements in telemedicine, cloud computing, and secure online payment systems provide an opportunity to address these challenges. Teledermatology enables remote consultations through image-based diagnosis and asynchronous communication, while online pharmacy systems streamline prescription fulfillment and inventory management. Integrating these components into a single platform can significantly improve the patient journey and overall healthcare outcomes.



\subsection{Project Description}
The proposed system is a comprehensive web-based platform that integrates an online dermatology clinic with an online pharmacy. The platform is designed to support the entire patient care lifecycle, beginning with user registration and continuing through consultation, prescription issuance, payment processing, and medication acquisition.

Patients can create secure accounts, upload dermatological images, describe symptoms, and book appointments with licensed dermatologists. Dermatologists can review patient submissions, provide diagnoses, conduct follow-up consultations, and issue digital prescriptions. These prescriptions are seamlessly linked to the integrated pharmacy module, allowing patients to view medication availability, pricing, and order status in real time.

The system also includes administrative features such as appointment management, inventory tracking, payment reporting, and user role management. Built using Next.js, Node.js, and PostgreSQL, the platform emphasizes scalability, security, and performance while ensuring compliance with healthcare data protection standards.



\subsection{Statement of the Problem}
Traditional dermatology clinics and pharmacies often operate as independent entities, relying heavily on manual and paper-based processes. This lack of integration leads to multiple inefficiencies that negatively impact both patients and healthcare providers.

Key problems include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty for patients to locate and book appointments with qualified dermatologists, particularly in non-urban areas.
    \item High administrative workload associated with manual patient registration, record keeping, and billing.
    \item Absence of a centralized digital system for tracking prescriptions, medicine availability, and payments.
    \item Ineffective communication mechanisms, resulting in missed appointments and delayed prescription fulfillment.
\end{itemize}

These challenges contribute to reduced service quality, increased operational costs, and limited scalability of dermatology services. Addressing these issues requires a unified digital solution that connects clinical and pharmaceutical workflows.

\subsection{Players of the Existing System (Roles)}
The existing healthcare system relies on several key actors:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient:} Visits the clinic physically, retains paper prescriptions, and coordinates separately with pharmacies.
    \item \textbf{Receptionist:} Manages manual registration, appointment scheduling, and paperwork.

\subsection{Current Activities and Workflow Analysis}
Current workflows are largely manual and fragmented, leading to inefficiencies:

\subsubsection{Patient Registration and Consultation}
Patients fill out physical forms during clinic visits. The receptionist records details in ledger books, which may result in:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Lost or incomplete patient data.
    \item Time-consuming retrieval of historical patient records.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Product Management and Stock Monitoring}
Pharmacists manually record inventory and visually inspect stock levels. Limitations include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Difficulty in tracking expiration dates.
    \item No automated alerts for low stock levels.
    \item Increased risk of stockouts or selling expired medicine.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Purchase Orders and Replenishment}
Purchase orders are communicated through phone calls or supplier visits. This leads to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Delays in procurement.
    \item Inefficient record-keeping of order history.
    \item Lack of accountability for suppliers.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Reporting and Communication}
Reports are handwritten, and communication relies on phone calls or verbal instructions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item High potential for errors in financial and inventory reporting.
    \item Missed appointments due to lack of automated reminders.
    \item Delayed communication with patients regarding prescriptions or test results.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Forms and Reports of the Existing System}
Current documentation relies heavily on paper, including:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Patient registration forms.
    \item Prescription sheets.
    \item Medicine stock cards and ledgers.
    \item Daily/monthly sales summaries.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Design and Construction}

\section{Introduction}
This chapter focuses on the detailed design and construction of the proposed integrated dermatology and pharmacy web platform. The chapter covers actor identification, business rules, system decomposition, dynamic behavior via sequence diagrams, data modeling, architectural design, user interface design, and security considerations. The aim is to provide a comprehensive blueprint for implementation, ensuring that the system is modular, scalable, and maintainable.

The design approach follows standard software engineering principles, emphasizing **modularity, separation of concerns, usability, security, and extensibility**. The chapter also integrates UML diagrams to provide a visual understanding of system components, interactions, and data flows.

\section{Actor Identification}
Actors are individuals or systems that interact with the platform. The correct identification of actors is crucial to defining system boundaries and responsibilities.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient:} End-user seeking dermatological consultation and medicine procurement. Responsibilities include registration, profile management, image upload, appointment booking, and online payment.
    \item \textbf{Dermatologist:} Provides diagnosis, manages patient medical records, issues prescriptions, and monitors treatment progress.
    \item \textbf{Pharmacist:} Manages inventory, verifies prescriptions, fulfills medicine orders, and updates stock levels.
    \item \textbf{System Administrator:} Oversees user management, monitors system performance, ensures compliance, and generates operational reports.
\end{itemize}

Additional system actors include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Payment Gateway:} External system for processing card and mobile money payments.
    \item \textbf{Notification Service:} Handles SMS/email reminders for appointments and order updates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Business Rules}
Business rules define constraints and workflow policies for proper system operation. They ensure **consistency, security, and compliance**.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Appointments must be paid for before confirmation.
    \item Medicine orders require a valid, authenticated prescription reference.
    \item Appointment reminders are automatically sent 24 hours prior via SMS/email.
    \item Stock levels must be updated in real time to prevent overbooking or over-selling of medicines.
    \item Each patient profile must have a unique identifier, and all transactions must be logged for traceability.
    \item Sensitive medical images must be encrypted at rest and in transit.
\end{itemize}

\section{User Interface Design}
\subsection{Design Principles}
The platform adopts a **mobile-first responsive design**, ensuring usability across smartphones, tablets, and desktops.

    \item \textbf{Dermatologist Dashboard:} Patient queue, image submissions, consultation notes.
    \item \textbf{Pharmacy Dashboard:} Inventory alerts, prescription verification queue, order tracking.
    \item \textbf{Admin Dashboard:} User management, analytics, system logs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Security Considerations}
Security is central due to sensitive patient data and financial transactions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item HTTPS for all web communications.
    \item JWT-based authentication for user sessions.
    \item Role-based access control to enforce least privilege.
    \item Encryption of all sensitive fields (medical history, prescriptions, payment data) in the database.
    \item Regular audit logs for compliance and traceability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Design Rationale and Justification}
The chosen design reflects:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Modularity:} Each module can be developed and tested independently.
    \item \textbf{Scalability:} Layered architecture and cloud storage allow future expansion.
    \item \textbf{Maintainability:} Standardized coding practices and ORM usage (Prisma) facilitate updates.
    \item \textbf{User-Centric Approach:} Dashboards and notifications designed for usability by medical and non-technical staff.
    \item \textbf{Integration Capability:} Designed for future integration with AI-assisted diagnosis or national health systems.
\end{itemize}


\chapter{Conclusion and Recommendations}

\section{Summary of Findings}
The development of the "Online Dermatology Platform with Integrated Online Pharmacy" has demonstrated the feasibility and benefits of integrating specialized clinical services with pharmaceutical workflows. The platform effectively streamlines the patient journey from initial consultation to medication procurement, reducing the time and effort required for patients to access skincare services. By automating appointment scheduling, image management, and prescription handling, the system minimizes administrative errors and improves data accessibility for both patients and healthcare providers.

\section{Achievement of Objectives}
The project successfully met its predefined objectives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Online Registration and Profile Management}: Patients can securely create and manage their digital health profiles.
    \item \textbf{Appointment Booking System}: A robust scheduling mechanism allows for selecting dermatology services and available slots.
    \item \textbf{Remote Consultation Facilitation}: Secure medical image uploads enable dermatologists to perform asynchronous diagnoses.
    \item \textbf{Secure Payment Gateway}: Integration with Chapa provides a reliable mechanism for consultation fees and medicine purchases.
    \item \textbf{Pharmacy Module}: A dedicated inventory management system allows pharmacists to track stock and fulfill orders efficiently.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
Despite the successful implementation, several limitations were identified:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Internet Connectivity}: The system’s performance is highly dependent on stable internet access, which remains a challenge in certain regions of Ethiopia.
    \item \textbf{Physical Medicine Delivery}: While the platform handles orders and payments, the actual physical delivery of medication still requires a separate logistical infrastructure.
    \item \textbf{Integration with National HER}: The system currently operates as a standalone platform and is not yet integrated with national Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems.
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommendations and Future Work}
To further enhance the platform, the following recommendations are proposed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{AI-Assisted Diagnosis}: Incorporating machine learning models to assist dermatologists in identifying common skin conditions from uploaded images could improve diagnostic speed and accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Mobile Application}: Developing a native mobile application (Android/iOS) would provide a more seamless experience and enable push notifications for reminders.
    \item \textbf{Integration with Delivery Services}: Partnering with local delivery providers to automate the final leg of medicine procurement.
    \item \textbf{Blockchain for Prescriptions}: Implementing blockchain technology to ensure the immutability and security of digital prescriptions, preventing unauthorized modifications.
\end{itemize}
